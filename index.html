What is Maze Lab?

A pathfinding algorithm is a step-by-step recipe a computer follows to get from A to B. Computers can't "see" a whole map at once the way you can. They have to explore it one spot at a time. Different recipes explore in very different ways, and Maze Lab makes those differences visible.

01

Build a maze

Click and drag to draw walls, or let the lab generate a maze for you. Paint mud on cells that are slow to cross.

02

Pick a strategy

Some algorithms spread out evenly like ripples in a pond. Some dive deep down one corridor. Some make educated guesses about where the goal is.

03

Watch & compare

See every cell the algorithm checks, read along in plain English, then race up to four strategies against each other on the same maze.

waiting

    New maze

    Every racer gets an identical copy of your maze from the Explore tab, including the mud. They all take one step at a time, together.

    Scoreboard

    #AlgorithmExploredPathCostTime

    How the winner is chosen: the cheapest path wins. If two racers tie on path cost, the one that explored fewer cells wins. Time is how long each racer took on screen.

    Five ways to search a maze

    Each algorithm makes a different trade-off between speed, memory, and whether it can promise you the shortest route.

    V is the number of cells and E the number of connections between them. On a grid each cell has at most four neighbours, so E ≈ 4V.
